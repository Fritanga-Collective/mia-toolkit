# Welcome to the MIA Toolkit Blog

If you have ever opened a drawer full of hospital imaging CDs and wondered what
on earth to do with them, this blog is for you. We built **MIA Toolkit** to
solve exactly that problem, and this is the place where we will share what we
learn along the way.

## Why we built MIA Toolkit

Medical images belong to *you*. Yet they almost always arrive trapped on a pile
of fragile discs, each one wrapped in its own clunky viewer that may not even
run on a modern computer. When the discs are scattered and hard to open, two
bad things happen:

1. Scans get repeated, costing you time and money.
2. Doctors miss changes because they never saw the older picture.

We thought that was wrong. So we made a free, private desktop app that copies
every disc, inventories every study, and builds **one organized archive on a
single USB drive** your radiologist can open at once.

Some things we care about deeply:

- **Privacy first.** Your data never leaves your computer. No account, no cloud,
  no tracking.
- **Free, forever.** Individuals never pay. Donations and institutional
  licenses keep it that way.
- **Standards-compliant.** The archive is real DICOM, so it loads in any
  hospital PACS or viewer.

## What this blog will cover

Expect practical, plain-language guides, such as:

- How to open a hospital imaging CD when the disc's own viewer won't run.
- What DICOM, DICOMDIR, and PACS actually mean — without the jargon.
- How to spot a fake or failing USB drive *before* it eats your scans.
- Stories from families helping an aging parent organize years of imaging.

You can always grab the app from the [home page](/), and if a guide helped you,
consider [supporting the project](/support.html) so it stays free for the people
who can't pay.

> Your medical history belongs to you. Let's make it easy to carry.

Here is a tiny technical aside for the curious: the archive index is a file
literally named `DICOMDIR`, and every file MIA copies to your USB is verified by
checksum as it goes — so a failing drive is caught instead of silently
corrupting your scans.

Welcome aboard. More soon.
