You slide the hospital CD into your laptop, double-click the program it tells you
to, and… nothing. A cryptic error, a window that never opens, or a flat "this app
can't run on your PC." Frustrating — but your images are almost certainly fine.
The problem is the **viewer**, not the scan.

## Why the bundled viewer fails

Hospitals often burn a tiny viewer application onto the disc next to your images.
Those viewers were built years ago, usually **Windows-only**, and frequently
won't launch on a current Windows or any Mac. The good news: the actual medical
images sitting beside that viewer are in a standard format called **DICOM**, and
you don't need the disc's program to open them.

## Three ways to get to your images

1. **Use a modern DICOM viewer.** Install a free, current viewer and point it at
   the disc. It reads the same files the broken bundled program would have.
2. **Copy the files off the disc first.** Discs are slow and fragile; copy the
   whole disc to a folder on your computer, then open *that*. Faster, and you
   stop depending on the scratched original.
3. **Consolidate everything.** If you have more than one disc, this is the moment
   to gather them all into a single, organized archive instead of fighting each
   one separately.
